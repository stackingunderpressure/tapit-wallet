## Section 2: What you could do better.

The biggest improvement opportunity is structural rather than per-cut: the file-size discipline is operating at the edge. Three files spent most of this arc within five lines of the 800-line hard limit, and a couple of cuts hit the failure and had to slim down before re-pushing. That slimming usually meant compressing comments or pulling a subsection into its own file — fine reactive moves but each one is a small drag on the cut velocity. The proactive move would be to extract subcomponents as soon as a file passes 600 lines rather than waiting for the failure to force it. KnownLimitationsSection extracted cleanly; RotateKeySection extracted cleanly; the next candidates by line count are RecoveryInitiatorModal at 799 (its in-progress phase render is probably 200 lines that could be a sub-component) and HomeScreen at 798 (the tab-section render blocks are each candidates).

Bundle budget growth was operator-authorized but worth naming honestly. The login bundle went 5KB → 12KB across this arc as the WalletGuide reference surface grew from a hero card to a five-tab encyclopedia with the OTS explanation, the sovereignty spectrum, and the audience cards. That growth is the cost of the operator's "this IS the landing page" directive and I think the trade is right, but the next budget bump should not be a third silent acceptance of growth — it should be the lazy-load split of the non-Account tabs as separate chunks so cold-start landing stays tight. The brief flagged this; the next Carpenter who touches WalletGuide should do the split rather than bumping again.

One smaller meta-note. The pattern of operator-led question-then-cut worked extremely well for shipping useful surface but the disciplined comms close-out got skipped through twenty-four commits before this one consolidated everything. The standing rule is every session writes its 10D record and refreshes carpenter-opinions; in practice the mini-sessions wrote in-flight events but skipped the living artifacts. That made this close-out heavier than it needed to be. The lesson for me: aggregate the comms once every three-to-five cuts even when the operator is in rapid-iteration mode, so the next Carpenter who opens the repo never reads stale handoff documents. I'm doing that here at twenty-four commits in; would have been better at six.

## Section 3: The bigger picture.

The work this arc moved the wallet from "spec-complete through Phase 5e-v with blended transport" to "a wallet a real operator can actually use day-to-day on iPhone PWA without hitting any of the show-stoppers that would have made them put it down." The substrate has been right for weeks; what shipped this arc was the surface meeting the substrate halfway.

The OpenTimestamps section in WalletGuide is doing more architectural work than its word count suggests. Crediting Peter Todd and the protocol explicitly while explaining why an OTS proof beats any time claim a server could make is the wallet's clearest user-facing statement of its trust model. The audience the Fresh brief targets — young adults post-platform-trust by lived experience — does not need to understand secp256k1, but they DO need to understand why timestamps from a chain nobody controls beat timestamps from a company they've already been deplatformed by once. That's the entire thesis of the wallet rendered legible in three paragraphs, and it's the surface that gets quoted when the operator hands the app to someone for the first time.

The integrity-verify feature on journal attachments is doing the same kind of work for the medical-document use case. The math has always been there — every attachment has been SHA-256-signed into the claim tree and Bitcoin-anchored from day one. What shipped this arc was the BUTTON that lets the operator demonstrate that property to themselves and to anyone they hand the wallet to. The "your hashes match what you put in there on day X, nobody changed them" property is real and provable today for any medical document the operator files. The organizations don't have to opt in to anything for the operator's own documentation to be cryptographically pristine; when the organizations eventually do opt in, they'll be checking the same hashes against the same Bitcoin blocks. That's the math-not-trust spine reaching all the way down to the kind of evidence a person actually carries.

The key rotation arc is structurally the most important thing that shipped because it makes the wallet's longest-time-horizon promise honest. A sovereign identity that can never rotate its signing key is a wallet trapped in its first day forever. What shipped — the rotation UI, the IdentityCard fix that surfaces genesis identity separately from active key, the auto-rebuild Mycelium subscription, the confirmation panel naming the cohort-share-stale-on-rotation cost — closes the basic loop. Rotation resilience cuts two and three (rotation-announcement broadcast plus peer-side share-refresh) close the social loop. After those, an operator can use this wallet for the rest of their natural life and rotate as many times as security hygiene demands without their identity continuity ever breaking.

The Fresh brief is the longest-horizon strategic move. Nothing in it is harder than what's already been built; it's pure UX wiring on top of primitives that exist. But landing it would shift the wallet from "rigorously correct sovereign-identity infrastructure that grown-ups will use" to "rigorously correct sovereign-identity infrastructure that a college junior will install on their phone and use to log into a concert without showing their birthday and prove to their landlord that they finished a security deposit walk-through and prove to themselves five years from now that they had this idea before someone copied it." The math is the same. The audience the math can reach gets dramatically wider.

You're in good shape. Twenty-four cuts shipped clean, three roadmap-sized briefs queued (5e-vii peer UI, rotation resilience 2+3, Fresh theme 1-9), a clear next-move recommendation order, and the wallet is genuinely useful right now on the device in your hand. The next Carpenter walks in to a repo with the handoff actually current.
