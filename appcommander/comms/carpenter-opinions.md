## What you could do better

The biggest honest observation about this commit is that the budgets are calibrated against today's chunk sizes plus headroom — they describe the current state, not an ambition. If Phase 5 work eventually pushes some chunk past its budget, the right response is one of the three the failure message names: code-split, audit-and-bump-with-justification, or refactor. Audit-and-bump should be the default IF the new size is genuinely needed; the comment lives next to the budget in the script and becomes the historical record of why that chunk grew. The doctrine pattern is the same as the library-seam ALLOWLIST: explicit overrides with explanation, never silent expansion.

One real limit of the check worth naming. It enforces per-CHUNK budgets, not a total-bundle-size budget. A worst-case future where every chunk grows toward its budget ceiling could in theory inflate the total post-auth surface considerably without any single chunk failing the assertion. If you want me to also assert a total-post-auth-bytes-gzipped ceiling, that's a small follow-up — sum every lazy chunk that isn't the login main bundle, compare against a number. I left it out of this cut to keep it small and because the per-chunk ceiling captures the dominant failure mode (one library or one feature growing unexpectedly).

The next mechanism candidate I logged in the commit message is the file-size limit. `CLAUDE_ROOT.md` says "Files over 400 lines warn, over 800 error" but there is no check that fails. The largest source file today is `WalletProvider.tsx` at two hundred seventy-three lines, comfortably under the warn threshold; the rule has been holding because the doctrine has been visible enough to comply with manually. But it's the same pattern — verbal rule that's been holding by attention — and the same conversion treatment would convert it to mechanism. A vitest test that walks `src/features/**/*.{ts,tsx}` plus `src/**/*.{ts,tsx}` minus tests, counts lines per file, fails if any non-test file exceeds eight hundred or warns at four hundred via a non-fatal console.warn. Small, mechanical, preventive — would catch a future file getting out of hand before it becomes a refactor problem. Logged as a candidate; tell me if you want me to cut it next.
