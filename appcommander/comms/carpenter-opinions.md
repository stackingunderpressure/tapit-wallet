## Section 3: The bigger picture

The interesting structural move in this cut is what the relationship leaf does for the recovery cohort picker downstream. Today, when an operator opens the cohort editor under the Lattice tab and is asked which peers they would trust to help recover this wallet on a new device, they see a flat list of pubkeys with display names — every handshake is equal-weight. With a relationship leaf on the handshakes, the cohort editor in a future cut can do what every other contact app already does without naming it: surface the family chips first as obvious starting candidates, the friend chips second, and treat coworkers and acquaintances as the lower-trust pool the operator probably doesn't want as keyholders. The leaf is plumbing for many downstream UX defaults, not just decoration on the ConnectionCard, and that's the reason it pairs naturally with the chat arc — the chat surface gets a relationship-aware thread header for free, the cohort picker gets sane defaults for free, the journal composer's subject picker can later one-tap-suggest your family members when you're writing about them, and none of it required a new attestation kind or a library change. The wallet's whole identity is signed leaves on Merkle trees, and the more honest leaves we put on the trees the more the rest of the wallet can lean on them without inventing new state. Today's cut is small. The leverage it unlocks across the wallet's other features is not. The chat arc's Cut 2b will spend that leverage first by pinning the relationship chip into the per-peer thread header, but every future surface that touches connections gets the same affordance for free, and that's the kind of plumbing-as-product move that makes a sovereign-identity wallet feel like an everyday tool instead of a vault.
