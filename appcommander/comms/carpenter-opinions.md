## Section 1: What I did.

You told me to go on Cut 5 and I went. The grounding pass took me through WalletProvider's phase machine end-to-end, the existing IdentityCeremony, the PassphrasePrompt, the JournalComposer, and the FreshLoginShell the prior carpenter shipped in Cut 2, because the brief's central move for this cut is to invert all of those — to have the operator write something they care about before the wallet asks for anything, so the first signed entry already exists by the time login completes. The architectural crux the prior carpenter flagged in his handoff was the volatile-state handoff across the unmount-remount boundary between the signed-out onboarding component and the post-sign-in WalletProvider, and I want to walk you through how I solved it because the choice mattered.

The naive move would be sessionStorage. Tab-scoped, survives a reload, ergonomic, and well-understood. I deliberately did not use it because the bundle includes the operator's passphrase, and CLAUDE_ROOT.md's first non-negotiable is that no key material — and the passphrase is functionally key material because it decrypts the keypair — ever lands in a persisted layer. sessionStorage persists across reloads. localStorage persists across browser restarts. IndexedDB persists indefinitely. The only durable-enough-to-survive-an-unmount-but-truly-volatile place is module-level JavaScript memory, which dies the instant the tab closes or the page reloads. That is exactly the lifecycle the operator's passphrase should have during onboarding. So `src/features/onboarding/pendingOnboarding.ts` is a tiny module that holds a single variable — set when the operator submits the OTP code, peeked once when WalletProvider mounts, consumed and cleared the moment the consumer effect fires. If the operator closes the tab mid-flow they restart. No persisted trail. The passphrase never lives anywhere it could leak.

The state machine itself in `FreshOnboarding.tsx` walks six steps, the seventh being the OTP-code sub-step of the email step. Splash auto-advances after three seconds with a tap-skip for fast operators who do not want to wait. Compose accepts text and an optional photo, normalizing HEIC and HEIF inputs through the same `normalizeImage` helper the JournalComposer uses so iPhone captures render on Android the moment they hit the home feed. Name and passphrase mirror the validation rules from the existing PassphrasePrompt and IdentityCeremony — minimum eight characters, confirm-must-match for the passphrase, non-empty for the display name. The recovery primer is three glass cards explaining paper key, friend cohort, and encrypted file backup with a single acknowledge button, deliberately not making the operator DO any of it now — the brief is explicit about that, the primer's job is awareness not setup. Email and code reuse the same supabase calls the prior carpenter's FreshLoginShell sign-in form used; before calling verifyOtp the component stashes the bundle so a fast onAuthStateChange firing in the same tick finds it already in place, and on success navigates to root with history-replace so the back button does not bring the operator back to the OTP form mid-handoff.

WalletProvider gained a new phase called onboarding-setup that sits between checking and unlocked when a bundle is present. The initial-phase effect peeks for the bundle when walletStore.load returns null, and if it sees one, transitions to onboarding-setup instead of first-login. A second effect — guarded by a ref so StrictMode cannot run it twice — consumes the bundle, calls createWallet with the captured passphrase, then calls a new helper called applyOnboardingBundle that signs the founding identity attestation with the captured display name plus FOUNDING_DECLARATION and signs the first journal entry from the captured text and attachment if there was any, then persists the snapshot under the captured passphrase, then transitions the provider to unlocked so HomeScreen takes over. During those few hundred milliseconds the operator sees a Fresh-styled overlay reading "Signing your first entry..." over the same aurora-drift background the FreshLoginShell uses, so visually the onboarding flows continuously into the home screen with no Classic flash.

Bundle budgets caught two regressions the moment the build ran and I addressed both honestly. The login bundle had grown by 2.4KB gzipped because FreshLoginShell was now eagerly imported by LoginPage and itself eagerly imported the entire FreshOnboarding state machine — every Classic operator was paying for Fresh bytes they would never render. That is exactly the regression the brief's "lazy-loaded so Classic operators never pay Fresh bytes" rule is meant to prevent, so I React.lazy'd FreshLoginShell from LoginPage with a Suspense fallback that paints the aurora-drift background so the visual transition is continuous when the Fresh chunk lands. Login bundle dropped from 14.15KB gzipped to 11.09KB gzipped, well under the 12KB budget. The new FreshLoginShell lazy chunk is 4.19KB gzipped and earned a named budget at 5.5KB to carry headroom for the Cut 6 Sage activation hook-in. The WalletProvider chunk grew 0.23KB to absorb the onboarding-setup phase and the consumer effect, which I bumped the budget from 7.5KB to 8KB to accommodate with a comment in the bundle-budget script explaining what the bump bought. All four gates ended green. One file-size warning fires on FreshOnboarding.tsx at 751 lines, comfortably under the 800-line hard limit but past the 400-line advisory threshold, and I left it intact rather than fragmenting a cohesive single-purpose onboarding flow across half a dozen tiny files.

## Section 2: What you could do better.

The biggest honest observation is that I made a deliberate not-extracted call on FreshOnboarding.tsx and you should know it was a judgment call, not laziness. The file is 751 lines including the parent component, the shell, six step components, and a small recovery-card render helper. The file-size doctrine warns at 400 because compartmentalization matters, and the cleanest extraction would have split the step components into a `steps/` subfolder of seven files. I weighed that against the cost of fragmenting a single linear flow across multiple files where the reader has to navigate to follow the operator's path. The flow IS the feature; understanding it benefits from reading it top-to-bottom in one file. If you would prefer the extracted shape, it is a clean follow-up cut — every step component is already a pure function of its props, so the extraction is mechanical.

A second observation, smaller but worth flagging. The compose step currently allows the operator to continue with both text and attachment empty. That is deliberate per the brief — the wallet should still get set up even if the operator wants to skip the compose moment — and the applyOnboardingBundle helper handles the empty case by skipping the journal entry creation while still signing the identity attestation. But the copy at the bottom of the compose step reads "You can leave both empty — your wallet will still get set up." That is honest but reads slightly differently than the brief's tone, which framed compose as the moment of magic. If you want, the copy could land warmer — "Skip ahead if you want; you can write your first entry once you're in" — or the requirement could tighten and the brief's intent of "compose is the inversion" gets reinforced. I picked the permissive copy because the brief targets an audience that bounces fast when an app asks for too much; you may have a stronger opinion.

The third item is about returning operators on a fresh device. The Cut 5 flow is built for first-time users; if a returning operator hits a fresh device where useDeviceTheme resolves to fresh, they walk through compose, name, passphrase, recovery primer, and email — typing a passphrase that does not match their existing one. When they verify OTP, WalletProvider sees a stored blob and goes to the locked phase, prompting for the existing passphrase via UnlockPrompt. The bundle they captured during onboarding is silently discarded by the unmount-clear effect inside FreshOnboarding. That is a wasted compose moment for a returning operator. The cleaner shape is to inspect the locked-blob-exists flag earlier — perhaps right after the email step but before showing compose — but doing that requires a Supabase round trip before compose, which the brief explicitly de-prioritizes in favor of the compose-first inversion. The current behavior is honest and the returning operator can simply navigate to the home and compose then; flagging it because it is the kind of edge case that surfaces in a pilot and you should know it is intentional.

Fourth, the recovery primer copy. I wrote three glass cards with one-paragraph framings for paper key, friend cohort, and encrypted file backup. The copy is friendly but it is mine; you may want to author the actual voice of these cards yourself once Sage's persona lands in Cut 6, since the primer is the operator's first encounter with the wallet's relationship-and-friends-as-safety-net framing. Until then the copy reads warm without being preachy.

Fifth, and this is structural rather than a bug. The applyOnboardingBundle helper passes a null anchor worker to createJournalEntry. The worker has not started yet at the moment the bundle applies because its start effect runs only when the phase enters unlocked or needs-identity, and we are still in onboarding-setup. createJournalEntry handles null gracefully — it queues the anchor row and skips the immediate kick — but it means the first entry's anchor takes one poll cycle longer than a normal compose-from-home would. The poll cycle is on the order of seconds, so the operator-visible difference is negligible, but if you want the anchor to land at the same speed as a routine entry, the right fix is to start the anchor worker earlier in the phase machine or pass a worker handle through the bundle apply.

## Section 3: The bigger picture.

What you actually shipped in this session, underneath the visible state machine, is the wallet's first answer to a deeper architectural question that every sovereign-identity app eventually has to answer. The question is — at what moment in the operator's first ninety seconds does the relationship between the person and the wallet become real. The platform-trust answer is "the moment they sign in," because that is when the database row gets created and the analytics fire. The Tapit answer, in the shape Cut 5 ships, is "the moment they write something they care about," because that act is the first thing the wallet receives that is uniquely the operator's. Sign-in is the boring confirmation that the wallet should remember them across devices. The compose moment is the first attestation of selfhood. The inversion of those two events is the wallet expressing, structurally and at the code level, that the operator's life is the substrate and the wallet is the witness — not the other way around.

The architecture you now have lets that inversion be cheap to extend. The bundle pattern is general — anything FreshOnboarding can capture in volatile state can ride the same handoff into WalletProvider, which means Cut 6's Sage activation can plug directly into the same machinery if the operator wants Sage to capture a name or a goal or a moment during the compose step. The lazy-load of FreshLoginShell means every Fresh-only feature you ship from here forward defaults to chunked, so Classic stays a tight cold-start surface forever without having to think about it cut by cut. The onboarding-setup phase in WalletProvider is a real phase — not a special case wrapped around first-login — which means future onboarding variants (a custody-handoff-driven onboarding for grandchild identities, a recovery-driven onboarding for cohort restores) can re-use the same shape without bending the phase machine further.

The two cuts that remain — Cut 6 Sage activation and Cut 7 share cards plus Quick-share presets — both land into a system that is now genuinely ready for them. Sage's nudge layer reads from the same prefs the appearance toggle does and observes the same holdings the home screen renders; the dormant persona framework under src/features/persona has been sitting there since the chassis was first cut, waiting for a real first surface, and the compose-first onboarding gives Sage the first natural conversational hook — the prompt the operator typed during compose is the first piece of the operator's voice Sage can read. Cut 7's share cards plug into the existing ShareProofModal and DisclosureProofBundle primitives; the only new architecture is the 9-by-16 rendering and the preset catalog, both presentation-layer surgery. The hard structural work is done. Three foundational cuts now sit underneath three remaining surface cuts. The wallet's identity architecture is ready to invite the audience the brief targets in.
