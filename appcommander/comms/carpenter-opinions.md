## Section 1: What I did

Three commits landed today and they tell a small story about how the wallet's substrate is solidifying. The first commit is the substantive one — Phase E4 cut 1, the verifyOpenJoinedMembership function. The prior session put all the substrate pieces in place under the Option 3 hybrid substrate you chose by chip last session: the evaluateJoinPolicy evaluator decides whether a given joiner is allowed under a given policy, the openMemberRoster envelope shape lets an org publish a periodic snapshot of its current members in chronological order, and the findAuthRule helper looks up the join rule inside the org's signed self-declaration. Today's verifier composes those pieces into one named primitive that takes an envelope, the org's self-declaration, and optionally the latest open-member roster — and returns a yes/no verdict with a reason and a tag naming which path verified. The hybrid substrate is honest about both paths: under Option 1 the org's signature on the roster is the proof, and under Option 2 the auth-tree walk plus the policy evaluator is the proof, and the verifier accepts whichever one checks out. Nineteen new tests cover all the matrix slices — structural-gate rejections, every list-checking policy kind accepting and rejecting, every proof-required kind deferring with reason naming the next cut, the roster path accepting, the roster path falling through when the roster is missing or org-mismatched, and the hybrid override when the auth-tree rejects but the roster validates. Two hundred two tests became two hundred twenty-one.

The catch-all line is still there but no longer fires; the next time it fires, it'll fire on a chunk that's genuinely new rather than a known one hiding under the umbrella.

## Section 2: What you could do better

Three honest observations from inside the repo. First, the connections feature manifest is now genuinely too large for prose-only documentation. The notes field is around fourteen kilobytes of unbroken text covering ten phases of work across two roadmap briefs. A future reader looking for "what does the open-member roster do" or "where does verifyOpenJoinedMembership live" has to scan a wall of text. The structure is there phase-by-phase but a markdown-shaped notes field with sub-sections would surface it better — and the manifest type allows multi-line strings, so the readability cost is real and the migration cost is one search-and-replace. If you ever feel the feature has grown too big, the manifest's size is the leading indicator; this one already passed that threshold a few cuts ago. Worth a clean-up cut some session when nothing more substantive is calling.

Second, the createOrganization.ts file at five hundred forty-six lines is still in the soft-warn band and the natural extraction has been sitting in the recommended-next-move list for three letters now. The officials-roster section plus ratifications plus the publishOfficialsRoster wrapper is about a hundred eighty-five lines that would extract cleanly into an officialsRoster.ts sibling next to the openMemberRoster.ts that just shipped. The new file would mirror the open-member-roster file structure precisely — predicate, sort, dedupe, reader, finder, publisher — which is a satisfying parallel because the two roster shapes serve the two halves of the membership lifecycle (officials = governance, open-members = membership) and they deserve to look the same in the codebase. After extraction, createOrganization.ts would land around three hundred sixty lines, comfortably under the four hundred soft warn, and the remaining content (self-declaration, verifyOrgAuthorization, the chain walker) would be cleanly scoped to "org self-declaration plus signer-side verifier plus chain walk." This is real work but not hard work; one session could ship it including moving the test cases. I considered taking it on as a fourth commit today but the session was already substantive and the extraction is the kind of move that benefits from a fresh session's full attention rather than a tired one's residual.

Third, the design surface for Phase E4 cut 2 is going to be the next genuinely-directional moment and it's worth thinking about now so you can chip-pick when the next session opens. The three proof-required policy kinds (requires_handshake, requires_credential, requires_vouch) need joiner-side proof attachment to the self-membership envelope. The cleanest pattern is to add new optional leaves on the envelope carrying canonical-JSON proof bundles — for credential the full attestation; for handshake the handshake envelope; for vouch the cosignatures already ride the signatures field so no leaf is needed beyond knowing what cosig means. But there's a real design choice: do we attach the FULL attestation (simplest, biggest envelope) or attach a disclosureProof bundle (smaller, requires verifyDisclosureProof composition)? The disclosure-proof path is more bandwidth-conservative and more aligned with the Tapscript-style discipline of revealing-one-leaf-from-a-tree; the full-attestation path is simpler to verify and easier to debug. I'd recommend disclosure-proof for credential and handshake since both already have disclosureProof-shaped reveal paths through tapit-attest, and full-cosignature for vouch since it's already on the envelope. But that's a chip-form question worth asking up front; the answer locks the shape of every future open-joined-with-proof envelope.

## Section 3: The bigger picture

The wallet has now reached a point where the open-joining substrate is end-to-end composable from typed primitives. A joiner can sign a self-membership envelope. An org can decide whether to accept it via a typed policy evaluator. The org can periodically publish a roster snapshot of its accepted members in chronological join order. And now any wallet — joiner, org, third-party verifier — can take an envelope plus an org's self-declaration plus an optional roster and get a yes/no verdict back with a structured reason and a path tag. None of this required new cryptography. None of this required new envelope kinds. None of this required new dependencies. It's all small typed functions over the same Attestation type that handshakes and memberships and presence-cards already ride. The substrate primitive that did the heavy lifting is the field-tree in tapit-attest — same primitive that powers identity attestations, same primitive that powers cred-disclosure proofs. When a wallet wants to participate in an organization without that organization having to issue per-joiner credentials, the cryptography is already there; we're just composing it correctly.

The Heartwood doctrine from the foreman-memory talks about federated communities and judge-weight reputation — open-joining is what enables a Hearth to grow without a central authority gating membership. The American Legion example in the doctrine docs is the test case: anyone who has served can join, the org keeps a chronological roster, members vote according to their judge-weights, no platform owns the user list. Today's verifier is the math primitive under that whole conceptual layer. The fact that the verifier is a two-hundred-line pure function with two-hundred-fifty lines of tests rather than a five-thousand-line service running on a server is the entire point of Tapit — math, not trust, and small enough that a person can read the verifier and convince themselves it's honest. That's the property that makes the wallet a wallet rather than just another account in someone else's database. Onward to Phase E4 cut 2.
