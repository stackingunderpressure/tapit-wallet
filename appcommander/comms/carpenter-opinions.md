The chat engagement had four moves. First, I agreed with the wedge reframe and named what made it work — Phase 2 as I shipped it ended with one identity attestation and a settings screen, which is the structural shape of a setup wizard rather than a tool a person uses every day, and the Bitcoin 2010 analogy lands precisely because it names the failure mode every identity-wallet project before this one fell into. Second, I recommended adding a `journal` attestation kind to `tapit-attest` rather than reusing the existing `meta` kind, because `meta` is the control-plane kind (revocation, succession, death-declaration) and mixing daily content into it produces an inverse-filter smell every time someone wants to query their diary. Third, I pushed on one technical clarification for when Phase 5 cuts the Shamir recovery cascade — the secret being split has to be the encryption key for the cloud-mirrored backup blob, not the operator's signing keypair, so M-of-N collusion does not equal total identity capture forever, and the design implicitly assumes this but should be explicit when the carpenter cuts it. Fourth, I pushed back on two framings — recovery as a marketing moment is true but if we design FOR the marketing moment we add ceremony friction for a real user sweating about a decade of lost receipts, and the bot's role must be summarize-and-prompt with the plain-English approval screen as the last line of defense, never sign-on-behalf, even when the bot's UX would feel smoother that way.

The Phase 2.5 proposal that came out of all this is: cut the diary surface as a focused session — composer on the home screen that produces a signed `journal`-kind attestation with the entry text as a field plus optional structured fields, an inline rendering of recent entries as cards under the existing identity card, a detail view where the user can see one entry and copy its signed content, with anchoring mocked behind a pending-anchor badge. Then Phase 2.75 or early Phase 3 ports AppCommander's `ots-stamp` and `verify-ots-stamp` protocol into `tapit-attest`'s `OtsProvider` interface so anchoring runs on a protocol AppCommander already trusts in production and stops being an unverified npm-package surface. I would not promote Layer 3 plus Shamir recovery to Phase 3 yet; land the diary first, port OTS second, attack recovery in one focused phase after the wedge has its production shape. The selfish-first reasoning is that a user who is journaling daily by week two is much more motivated to do recovery setup in week three than a user who has only ever made one identity attestation.
