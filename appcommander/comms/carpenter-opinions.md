# Carpenter opinions — tapit-wallet

## What I did

You gave me two things in one breath: the magic-link login is not being remembered and makes you redo it every time, and the name attestation needs to stop being a bare form field and become a real guided moment — the birth of an identity, with the responsibility made plain. I cut both as two separate clean commits, and I want to walk you through what was actually broken and what each fix does, because the first one in particular had a root cause that is worth understanding.

Cut A, commit `4f1dd7f`, is the login fix. Here is what was really happening. The old `LoginPage` sent a magic link — an email with a clickable link that, when tapped, completes the sign-in. The problem is iOS specific and it is brutal. When you tap a link inside an email on an iPhone, it opens in Safari. It does not open in the installed Tapit Wallet PWA, because as far as iOS is concerned the PWA and Safari are two different apps with two different, walled-off storage scopes. So the magic link completes the Supabase sign-in, the session token gets written — into Safari's storage. Then you switch back to the installed wallet PWA, and the PWA looks in its own storage scope, finds nothing, and asks you to log in again. Every single time. The session was never lost; it was just being filed in a drawer the wallet cannot open. The fix is to never leave the PWA at all. The new `LoginPage` is a two-step flow: you type your email, Supabase emails you a six-digit code, and you type that code back into a second screen inside the wallet. Under the hood the first step calls `signInWithOtp` and the second calls `verifyOtp` with `type: 'email'`. Because `verifyOtp` runs inside the PWA, the session token it produces gets written into the PWA's own storage scope — the same drawer the wallet reads from on the next launch. The login persists. The code screen also has a resend button and a use-a-different-email escape hatch, and I deliberately kept the `/auth/callback` route alive as a fallback so that if anyone does click the link instead of typing the code, it still works. One thing you must do on your side for this to function at all: the Supabase "Magic Link" email template has to include the `{{ .Token }}` variable, because that is what puts the actual six-digit code into the email body. Right now that template probably only has the confirmation URL. Add `{{ .Token }}` to it — the template can keep the URL too, the fallback still uses it — and the code path is live.

Cut B, commit `e52603e`, is the identity ceremony. The old flow was a single screen with one text input asking for a display name, and then it signed an identity attestation. You wanted that moment to carry weight, and you were right to. This is the root of the entire chain — every attestation the person ever holds descends from this one identity. So I built `IdentityCeremony.tsx`, a four-screen walk. The first screen, welcome, tells the person plainly what just happened: a keypair was generated that exists nowhere else in the world, no company holds a copy, and that means the responsibility is theirs — keep the passphrase, make a backup, the sovereignty is real and so is the duty. The second screen takes the name — a required display name, what people call them, and an optional full legal name for the founding record. The third screen is the one that makes it a ceremony rather than a form: it shows a founding declaration as a quoted statement — "I am creating this identity of my own free will. These keys are mine. I am responsible for protecting them. What I sign with them is mine, and mine alone." — and the person has to actively check a box affirming they have read it and affirm it before the Sign button enables. The fourth screen is the brief signing acknowledgement. The important part underneath: that declaration is not just UI text. It is signed as a leaf on the identity attestation, alongside the display name, the optional full name, the public key, and the creation timestamp. The person did not tap through a wizard — they signed a statement, and that statement is now a permanent, verifiable part of their record. I also raised the identity attestation's tier from `routine` to `notable`, because the founding identity is categorically not a routine receipt. The old single-field `DisplayNamePrompt` is deleted; nothing references it anymore, grep confirmed clean.

Both cuts passed all four gates after each commit — typecheck, lint, test at nineteen of nineteen, and build with the bundle budgets all under their caps. The file-size guardrail from last session confirms `IdentityCeremony.tsx` at roughly two hundred seventy lines sits comfortably under the four-hundred-line warn tier. The wallet-core manifest was updated in the same commit that shipped the feature, per doctrine, swapping `DisplayNamePrompt.tsx` for `IdentityCeremony.tsx` in the touches array.

## What you could do better

The honest gap this session is that Cut A is correct-by-construction but not yet confirmed-in-the-field, and the part that closes that gap is on your side, not mine. The login fix depends on the Supabase email template carrying `{{ .Token }}`. I cannot edit your Supabase project, I cannot send myself a test email, and I cannot walk the flow on a real iPhone. So the entire chain — type email, receive code, type code, verify inside the PWA, close the app, reopen it, still logged in — is unverified until you walk it. The logic is sound and the root-cause diagnosis is solid, but please do not treat this as done until you have actually force-quit the PWA and reopened it to a logged-in wallet. If the code does not arrive in the email, the template is missing the token variable.

A smaller observation worth naming: the new code screen accepts a `maxLength` of eight on the code input even though the code is six digits, which I left intentionally loose because Supabase token formats have varied and a too-tight input is a worse failure than a slightly loose one — but if you ever see a verification fail on a correct-looking code, the input length is the first thing to rule out. It is a deliberate choice, not an oversight, but it is the kind of thing that is invisible until it bites.

The third thing is the same standing item I have flagged before and will keep flagging until it clears: the operator-verification debt. The v1 surface is now feature-complete — login, identity ceremony, the diary, photos, cloud backup, QR, the anchor worker, the guardrails — and all of it carries gate-level confidence only. Gate-level means the code compiles, lints, tests pass, and it builds. It does not mean the buttons do what a human expects when a human taps them on a real phone against the real Netlify and Supabase deploy. The `PLAN.md` verify checklist plus the cut-specific walks in recent comms are the sequence. That walk is the single highest-value thing you can do next, and it is the thing only you can do.

## The bigger picture

These two cuts are small in line count but they are both about the same deeper thing: the seam where a sovereign-identity system meets an actual human being. Cut A is a reminder that sovereignty is worthless if the front door sticks. A wallet that holds keys nobody can take away, but that makes you re-authenticate every time you open it, fails the person in the most ordinary way possible — friction at the threshold. The iOS storage-scope split is not a glamorous bug, but fixing it is exactly the Prime Directive in practice: build the smallest useful version correctly, and useful means it actually opens. Cut B is the other half of the same coin. The founding declaration being signed as a cryptographic leaf rather than printed as decoration is the whole philosophy of this wallet in miniature — a statement a person affirms becomes a verifiable fact, not because a server recorded it, but because they signed it with a key only they hold. The ceremony is not theater. It is the moment the chain of custody for a human life's verifiable record actually begins, and dressing it with the weight it deserves is honest, not ornamental. You asked for it to feel special and to convey the responsibility; the reason that instinct was right is that the responsibility is genuinely the price of the sovereignty, and a person should meet both at the same moment. The wallet is now feature-complete enough to stop building and start verifying — and that handoff, from the Carpenter's gates to your hands on a real phone, is itself the loop closing.
