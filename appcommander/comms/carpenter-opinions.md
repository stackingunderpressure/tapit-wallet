## Section 2: What you could do better.

Two honest pieces of feedback from inside the repo. The first is about the journey-board UX I shipped at minimal fidelity. The per-peer rows in the awaiting phase show state — queued, sending, sent, received, send-failed, response-error — without distinguishing which transport carried the response or which transport the operator chose for the request. The threshold accumulates correctly regardless, which is the load-bearing property, but your framing was explicitly "you go on the journey," and journey deserves narration. A richer board would show, per peer, something like "Alice — sent via Nostr 8:42 — share received via Nostr 8:44" or "Bob — visited 9:15 — share received in person 9:17." The current minimal version works because the architecture is right; the operator-narrating-the-journey UI is the next coat of polish and worth its own small cut after the real-device walk surfaces what timing details actually matter most to read.

The second is the recovery-request QR exposing the operator's identity pubkey, name, and optional message as plaintext in the code. That is by design — the responder filter inside the modal is `view.ownerId === requestView.oldIdentity && view.shareFor === wallet.identity` so a stranger scanning the QR just gets no held share and nothing leaks cryptographically. But the operator showing the QR in a public place is putting their old identity pubkey and their full name on a code that any phone in eyeline can scan. Worth a small inline note in the Show request QR copy if the operator chooses to walk the recovery somewhere not-perfectly-private. Not a bug; just a UX honesty point.

Smaller meta-note: the doctrine names five cuts as the upper bound for a session, and this one shipped six feature cuts plus the original three-cut session plus their close-out commit — seven commits this branch this calendar day, six of them feature cuts. You authorized the extension explicitly with "go until session_ended" so the bound was respected as a guideline rather than a hard fence, and every gate stayed green throughout, but I want to flag it honestly because past five cuts the context fragmentation does start to show in the artifact quality. The first three cuts have richer test coverage than the last three. If a future session wants to ship as much, splitting it across two operator-authorized sessions with a fresh ground-state in the middle will produce tighter work than one long push.

## Section 3: The bigger picture.

The slime metaphor stopped being aspirational and became literal in this session. The math half of the cascade was already proven last session via the in-process round-trip test in `createRecoveryRequest.test.ts`. Phase 5e-v in the first half shipped the new-device choreography — operator on a fresh device clicks the recovery link on the locked screen, names their cohort, watches three of five peers release back to a ceremony pubkey that lives only for the modal, emerges with their wallet back under a new passphrase. The blended arc in the second half shipped the transport-agnostic choreography you named in your own words: the cousin two states away responds from her couch over Nostr, the neighbor next door responds from your kitchen over a QR you scan with your phone's camera, both shares land in the same pool, the same threshold accumulates, the same combine plus restore puts the wallet back. Three structural truths the work surfaced.

First, the envelope shapes are doing the load-bearing work and they were designed for this. The recovery-request and share-response envelope shapes from last session knew nothing about transports — they're tapit-attest credentials with signatures and NIP-44-encrypted-inside-payloads. The Mycelium path wraps them in NIP-44-at-the-event-layer plus Nostr event headers; the QR path encodes the same JSON as text the camera can scan. Same envelope, different lens. That's the architecture being kind to you — six cuts of UI wiring on top of a foundation that didn't need to change. The shared envelopeRoute module that Cut 4 extracted is the seam that lets future envelope kinds get blended-transport handling for free, as long as new envelopes go through the routing rather than building parallel dispatchers per arrival surface.

Second, the strict-verification gating gets stronger when the transport is in-person, not weaker. The verification step exists because the math says "if M peers cooperate, the secret reconstructs" but the protocol's social meaning depends on each peer personally verifying that the recovering operator is really who they claim. Over Nostr the verification has to happen out-of-band over a voice or video call — peer reads the ceremony pubkey aloud, operator confirms by reading it back. In person the peer is literally sitting next to the operator looking at the same phone — there is no out-of-band because there is no apart. The strict-verification checkbox guards both paths in the responder modal, and in the in-person case ticking it is just acknowledging what's right in front of you. That's the cleanest tier of identity verification the protocol has.

Third, the "lazy operator" framing you named — backups are the right answer but we are trying to help the lazy with great UX, nothing fills the gap all the way — is now closed about sixty to seventy percent by what shipped. Operators who declare a cohort and live on Mycelium got recovery in the first half of the session. Operators who declare a cohort and live entirely in-person now have recovery through the blended arc. Operators who do neither — single device, no cloud, no cohort, forgot the passphrase — still have no recovery path, and never will without either a paper backup or a peer holding a share. The remaining unconditional closure of the gap is paper-K_data: a Settings surface that exposes K_data as something the operator can write down once, plus a matching lock-screen import that types it back in to skip the whole ceremony. That pairs naturally with the blended cohort cascade — operators who do nothing get the cohort path; operators who do one small thing get an unconditional fallback. Both can ship; neither alone fully closes the gap.

The remaining Phase 5e piece — the peer-witnessed recovery-succession event from spec section twelve — should ride the same blended-transport doctrine from the first cut. Don't build it Mycelium-only and then retrofit; design both transports from the architectural starting line, because the cohort peers who responded with their shares are now the same cohort peers who will co-sign the succession event, and the same blend (some over Nostr, some in person) applies. Cut that as a fresh session with the architecture pre-decided.

You're in really good shape. Six cuts of feature work in one calendar day across two sessions plus a clean comms record, gates green at every commit, the slime real in code through both transports, and the lazy-operator gap genuinely meaningfully closed. The next session walks in with two concrete pieces — 5e-vii peer-witnessed succession and the paper-K_data export — and the doctrine for handling both is on the page.
