## The one-line

Bitcoin is the financial substrate of every Hearth, Lightning is the medium through which Hearths pay for compute and settle with each other, and the on-chain footprint of the network is minimized everywhere possible while verifiability stays cryptographically strong.

That is the whole spec in a sentence. Everything else is implication.

## Why Bitcoin and not something else

The operator's stated values from THE_THESIS.md determine this answer with no remaining ambiguity. Sovereign over rented means the financial layer cannot route through a corporation that can be debanked, censored, regulated into compliance with someone else's preferences, or rate-limited at scale. That eliminates ACH, Stripe, Square, PayPal, every credit card processor, every fiat-rail-dependent crypto wrapper, every stablecoin issued by a centralized entity, and every Layer 2 chain whose settlement guarantees ultimately depend on a multisig the operator did not sign. Free over paid means the medium itself cannot extract rent from the flow of value between operator and friend, between operator and LLM, between Hearth and Hearth. That eliminates every payment rail whose business model is taking a cut. Open over closed means the protocol must be readable, forkable, and continuable by anyone with a copy of the spec, with no committee owning future direction. Folk-scale over enterprise-scale means a family hub running on a Raspberry Pi must be able to send and receive value on the same rails as a hedge fund, with no asymmetry of access. Sleep-at-night ethics means the medium cannot be tied to ad-funded surveillance, cannot fund violence as its primary use case, cannot be issued by a counterparty whose solvency is a question. Bitcoin is the only money on the planet today that passes all six filters at once. Everything else loses on at least one. The operator's existing work in the family — DynastyTrust for sovereign multisig custody, Wealth Strategy for sat-acquisition and signal-driven accumulation, the bitcoin-maxi mode in Family Forge — already converges on this answer; this document just names it.

## Lightning as the LLM payment rail

The bet behind this whole architecture is that LLMs in the next generation will increasingly settle on Lightning rather than on credit cards billed to corporate accounts. The current state of LLM access in 2026 is that every API request flows through a corporate billing relationship with a small number of model providers, each of whom decides who gets access, at what rate, with what content restrictions, with what deplatforming risk, and with what surveillance footprint over the operator's prompts and outputs. That is the opposite of sovereign compute. Pay-per-call Lightning settlement removes every one of those failure modes at once. An operator's Hearth holds its own sat balance, points its engine at whatever LLM provider accepts Lightning that day, settles on a millisecond-grade payment channel for each inference, and switches providers freely with no account migration. The LLM sees no identity beyond the invoice; the operator owes no recurring subscription; the rate is what the market sets in the moment. The pieces required to make this real are mostly already shipped — Lightning works, hosted node providers exist, several open-weight model hosts already accept Lightning, and the LSP market is competitive. What is missing is the integration layer inside Hearths that turns this from a research curiosity into a default. That integration layer is what AppCommander as the reference Hearth eventually ships.

The implication for the rest of this network is unavoidable. A Hearth that cannot pay for its own compute is not sovereign in compute; it is renting compute from whoever holds the billing account. If a wife runs her family's Hearth on her husband's Stripe-billed Anthropic account, she is one bad day away from losing access to her own AI. If a homesteader runs a Pi-hosted Hearth on a credit card in their name, they are one debanking event away from losing access to their own AI. If a small church runs its members' Hearth on a 501c3-billed account, they are one regulatory letter away from losing access to their own AI. Every one of those failure modes evaporates the moment the Hearth holds its own sats. Sovereignty in compute requires sovereignty in payment, and sovereignty in payment requires Bitcoin held in the operator's own custody. There is no other architecture that closes this loop honestly.

## How Wealth Strategy fits into the network

Wealth Strategy is not just a personal-finance app inside the family of products the operator builds. It is the user-facing onramp to the financial-substrate layer of the Mycelium. Its job in the larger architecture is to teach every Hearth operator how to acquire sats, how to grow them at the pace their goals require, and how to allocate the resulting balance between custody (long-term storage) and operational liquidity (the wallet that funds the Hearth's compute). The Wealth Strategy approach to Bitcoin-cycle accumulation is the first half — getting more sats into the operator's hands. The DynastyTrust approach to multisig custody is the third half — keeping those sats safe across generations. The missing middle, which neither product currently owns directly, is the operational wallet layer that lives inside the Hearth itself and pays for inference on demand. That layer is what the eventual Hearth implementation will ship. Wealth Strategy and DynastyTrust together define the bookends; the Hearth is the middle.

The architectural framing this enables is important. Wealth Strategy is no longer just a product in the operator's portfolio. It is infrastructure. As more of the Mycelium comes online and more Hearths operate, the demand for the Wealth Strategy layer grows because every new Hearth needs an onramp to sats. The operator does not need to monetize Wealth Strategy with a per-user subscription to make this work; the product's value is that it is the smoothest path into the financial substrate that the rest of the network depends on. Other builders are explicitly invited to build alternative onramps. The architecture only succeeds if multiple onramps exist, the same way the architecture only succeeds if multiple Bench implementations exist.

## The anchor-don't-bloat principle

The operator's discipline on this is non-negotiable and worth preserving in his own framing. The chain is a public clock and a settlement layer, not a database. Money goes on-chain when settlement matters, with reasonable fee discipline. Hashes and commitments use OpenTimestamps batched anchoring, where a Merkle tree of thousands of receipts produces a single OP_RETURN write per batch and every receipt in the batch is independently verifiable from its inclusion proof plus the anchored root. Recipe content stays off-chain in the originating Hearth's storage. Attestations stay off-chain in both peers' Hearths and are exchanged directly. Identity profiles stay off-chain in the Hearth's local store. Memory entries, comms records, and session opinions stay off-chain. Only the cryptographic commitments that need a public clock get anchored, and they get anchored in batches that minimize the per-receipt footprint to a vanishing fraction of a satoshi-vbyte.

The reason this discipline matters is twofold. First, the network's growth depends on it being cheap. A Hearth running for years should generate kilobytes of on-chain footprint at most, not megabytes. The economics of folk-scale operation require that ten thousand Hearths anchoring honestly cost the network roughly the same as one Hearth operating profligately. OpenTimestamps batching is the existing solution to this exact problem and it works. Second, the integrity of the chain itself depends on the network as a whole respecting its purpose. Bloating the chain with content that does not need a public clock is a form of free-riding on the work bitcoin miners do to secure the network. The operator's stated values rule this out on ethical grounds even before they are ruled out on economic grounds. We anchor proofs of events without anchoring the content of the events. We use the chain's tamper-evidence as a clock, not its block space as a hard drive.

The implication for receipt design is that every receipt the Hearth produces — recipe shipped, transaction settled, attestation issued, work session completed — is hashed into a Merkle tree maintained by the Hearth, and the Hearth's anchoring service batches roots to OpenTimestamps on a configurable schedule. Daily anchoring is sufficient for most use cases; hourly anchoring is available for receipts that need tighter clock guarantees; per-receipt anchoring is rejected as an anti-pattern except in adversarial contexts where the per-event anchor is the entire point. Receivers verify a receipt by reading the inclusion proof against the anchored root, retrieving the Bitcoin block that contains the OP_RETURN, and confirming the timestamp. This pattern is fully established prior art and the Hearth simply implements it correctly.

## Privacy follows from what we don't anchor

The cryptographic discipline above is in service of privacy, not against it. Anchoring proof of an event without anchoring the content of the event means the world can verify that the operator said something on a particular date without seeing what they said until the operator chooses to reveal it. The hash of a recipe is anchored; the recipe stays in storage. The hash of an attestation is anchored; the attestation stays in two Hearths. The hash of a state snapshot is anchored; the state stays in the Hearth. Every commitment is verifiable; almost no content is exposed. The Mycelium can trace the geometry of trust paths and the timing of meaningful events, but the soil around the tap roots stays the operator's. This is the standard zero-knowledge commitment pattern and it does the work we need it to do.

The privacy property the operator most cares about is preserved by this discipline. The chain knows that something happened on a date. The chain does not know what happened, who it happened with, or what it meant. The Hearth knows everything; the chain knows commitments. The receivers of a recipe or an attestation know what they were given; the chain knows commitments. The federation knows the geometry of trust connections; the chain knows commitments. This is the right shape, and the rule that produces it is the same rule that minimizes bloat. Privacy and bloat-minimization converge on the same architectural decisions; we get both for the price of one.

## How AppCommander already implements parts of this

The reference Hearth as of 2026-05-10 implements roughly half of what this document describes and stubs the other half honestly. Identity has the keypair pattern but does not yet sign anything outbound for the network. Storage works but does not yet expose recipe export endpoints federated peers can hit. Engine is fully operational and currently bills through corporate Anthropic credits, which is the exact failure mode this document names — the eventual Lightning swap is the canonical Layer 4 ship that turns AppCommander into a fully sovereign Hearth instead of a partially sovereign one. Recipes exist in proto-form via the Frank skeletons but are not signed, not anchored, and not exchangeable across operators. Federation is not yet implemented. Anchoring is not yet implemented. The financial substrate work is therefore the largest single block of remaining work to elevate AppCommander from "operates like a Hearth in spirit" to "operates as a Hearth that the rest of the network can verify against." That work is queued behind the Layer 1 identity formalization that MYCELIUM.md names as next, and it constitutes the bulk of what the eventual SATOSHI roadmap looks like in concrete terms.

The build order that follows is a deliberate sequence that lets the financial substrate land in pieces without breaking anything. First the Hearth gets a real keypair the operator can see and export, completing Layer 1 identity. Second the Hearth gets a Lightning wallet primitive that the operator can fund manually from any source, with the wallet's balance visible in the cockpit and a sat-spend log per inference call. Third the engine layer gets a Lightning-payment adapter that swaps the Anthropic billing path for a sat-spend path when an LSP and an LLM endpoint that accept Lightning are configured, with the legacy corporate billing path preserved as fallback. Fourth the receipts layer gets OpenTimestamps batching, with a daily anchor cron and a verification UI that shows the inclusion proof for any past receipt. Fifth the federation layer gains the ability to settle peer-to-peer recipe transactions in sats when the originator has marked a recipe as paid and the receiver has approved the spend. Each step is a focused dispatch, each step ships value on landing, and the order ensures no step depends on infrastructure that does not yet exist.

## What other builders plug in

The architecture is open and the financial substrate is one of the most valuable seams to extend. Three explicit invitations stand from day one. Build an alternative wallet layer — the reference Hearth will ship a default Lightning wallet, but operators may want a different node implementation, a different LSP, a different custody model, a different fee strategy. As long as the wallet implements the substrate's settlement interface, it is a valid component. Build an alternative anchoring layer — OpenTimestamps over Bitcoin is the reference, but other commitment schemes that meet the bloat-minimization discipline are welcome and the architecture is intentionally agnostic. Build alternative onramps to sats — Wealth Strategy is the reference user-facing onramp, but the network needs many onramps with different educational philosophies, different signal engines, different relationships to traditional finance. The substrate succeeds if it is plural at every layer. Pull requests against this document, against any of the layers it specifies, and against any alternative implementation, are explicitly welcome from anyone implementing the spec honestly.
